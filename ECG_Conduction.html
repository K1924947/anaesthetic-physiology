Why the ECG looks like that

The heart is the cause, the leads are the effect. Teaching tool, not clinical guidance.

0 ms after the SA node fires

QRS 90 ms

For anaesthetist review
  • Activation timings are idealised round numbers for one beat at 75 per minute (PR 160 ms, QRS 85 ms normal, 140 ms in RBBB and LBBB), checked against Klabunde's sequence; not measured values.
  • Every lead trace is calculated as the projection of one idealised heart vector (a single dipole) on that lead's axis, so lead polarity always matches the arrow. The vector's components and timings are hand-set to reproduce the textbook patterns below. Real ECGs also contain non-dipole and electrode-proximity effects; chest leads use one shared gain. Not recordings.
  • The heart is a flat, simplified four-chamber cut-through; valve, vessel and conduction positions are schematic.
  • The arrow on the heart and the wheels show that same vector: frontal components (x, y) on the heart and frontal wheel, horizontal components (x, z) on the horizontal wheel; dashed during ST and T. Chest lead angles are standard approximations. On the diagram, repolarisation is shown only as a fade.
  • Time is stretched so one beat fills each panel: thin vertical lines are 40 ms, horizontal lines 0.5 mV. The ms readout and QRS width are true values.
  • Secondary ST and T changes in RBBB and LBBB are shown as typical patterns; individual patients vary.
