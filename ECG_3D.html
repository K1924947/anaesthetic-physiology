About this ECG

All 12 leads are recorded from one clock and laid out as a standard 12-lead page. The four columns show the same 2.4 s window at the same moment, each with a cursor at the same position in the column, moving in step. The lead II rhythm strip below shows a fixed 9.6 s loop, with its cursor at the matching time on that longer strip. Each lap starts on blank paper; the trace draws in behind the cursors, then the paper clears and the next lap starts from blank. The close-up and the heart are views of the same recording. The tracing is normal sinus rhythm at about 75/min, with mild respiratory sinus arrhythmia, so successive laps are not identical. 25 mm/s. Limb leads and the rhythm strip are at 10 mm/mV; chest leads V1 to V6 are at 5 mm/mV, as on many real ECG machines. A full-height 1 mV calibration pulse starts each row; a half-height 1 mV pulse marks the chest-lead columns, with the 5 mm/mV chest gain named in the caption rather than on the traces. The close-up of any lead is always at 10 mm/mV. One small square is 40 ms (0.1 mV at 10 mm/mV, 0.2 mV at 5 mm/mV). The model computes a normal adult tracing at 75 beats/min. About this model

  • This is a simplified teaching model, not a patient recording. The heart is made of about 10,000 small muscle elements. The impulse starts at the SA node, spreads through the atria cell to cell, is held in the AV node, races down the His bundle (through the top of the septum), the right bundle branch on the right side of the septum (with a branch across the moderator band to the right ventricular free wall) and the left bundle branch on the left side, which divides into anterior and posterior fascicles. The Purkinje fibres then spread over the apex and up the inner walls towards the base, and the wave crosses the ventricular wall from the inside (endocardium) outwards. In the atria, a fast band (Bachmann's bundle) carries the impulse from the right to the left atrium. The anterior, middle and posterior internodal routes are drawn from the SA node to the AV node, but are timed with the atrial muscle around them, because the atria conduct mainly cell to cell and the evidence for faster internodal tracts is only functional; so they do not change the P wave or the PR interval. The AV node sits low in the interatrial septum, in Koch's triangle near the opening of the coronary sinus. The fibrous AV ring at the valve plane insulates the atria from the ventricles, so the only way through is the AV node and His bundle. The drawn fibres are a simplified few; the model's Purkinje network covers the lower inner walls.
  • The picture uses the same code as the 2D ECG Conduction page: the fast route (His bundle, bundle branches, Purkinje fibres) is blue and travels along the lines; slow, cell-to-cell spread through muscle is orange hatching; muscle that is still depolarised is pale blue with blue dots; recovering muscle is grey dashes. Each line on the heart lights up between the model's own start and end times for that segment, the same times that make the 12-lead, so the heart, the arrow and the traces stay in step.
  • Each element adds a small dipole as its wavefront passes. Their sum at each moment is the mean electrical vector (the black arrow). The limb leads record the arrow's component along their own line; the chest leads are computed from the nearby muscle, because they sit close to the heart.
  • Conduction speeds follow published values for a real heart (atrial muscle about 0.5 m/s, AV node about 0.05 m/s, His and bundle branches about 2 m/s, Purkinje fibres about 4 m/s), scaled to the model. Shapes, sizes, the ventricular muscle speed and a slower His bundle (because the model heart is small, this gives a normal His-to-ventricle time of about 40 ms) are model choices, tuned so that the normal 12-lead falls within normal limits.
  • The limb leads use the hexaxial angles: I 0°, II +60°, aVF +90°, aVR −150° (positive angles point downwards). The augmented leads are drawn at 0.87 times the size of I and II. Chest electrodes sit on the chest wall of the torso outline: V1 at the 4th intercostal space, right sternal edge; V2 at the 4th intercostal space, left sternal edge; V4 at the 5th intercostal space, mid-clavicular line; V3 midway between V2 and V4; V5 at the anterior axillary line at the same level as V4; V6 at the mid-axillary line at the same level as V4.
  • Anatomy labels are anchored on the model, not by hand-tuned screen positions: chambers use the ellipsoid centroid or a point 0.6 of the way from that centroid to the surface along an anatomical direction (LV posterior-left towards the apex, RV anterior and to the patient's right, LA posterior and superior, RA anterior and to the patient's right, septum at its model centre). The SA node is at the model's SVC–RA junction, the AV node at the inferior interatrial septum, Bachmann's bundle and the His–Purkinje tree follow the drawn paths, and the Purkinje label sits at the mid-point of the left-ventricular apical fibre. The chambers themselves are tapered ellipsoids, and the conduction tree is a simplified few fibres, so a label marks a representative point on that structure, not a dissected heart. The internodal routes are schematic paths through the right atrial wall and the interatrial septum, the coronary sinus opening is a single marker just behind and below the AV node, and the fibrous ring is drawn on the cut face at the valve plane. These anchors should be reviewed by an anaesthetist, especially the AV node relative to the coronary sinus, the three internodal routes, the fascicle paths towards the papillary muscles, and where the moderator band meets the RV free wall.
  • The model beat runs at 75 per minute.
