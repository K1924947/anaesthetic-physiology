How scenarios work
  1. 1Pick a listed case, or choose Random scenario. Random draws from a larger pool that includes extra cases not shown in the list. The name of a random case is revealed in the debrief, not while you are playing.
  2. 2Read the patient and the monitor, then choose actions in the order you would in real life. Use Identify and treat reversible causes and Ask for / check when you need a list of causes or a test result. Each opens as a pop-up, so you do not have to scroll the actions list. Requested results, and Cycle NIBP now, appear after a short real-time delay and do not advance the simulated clock. You can keep treating while they are pending. Nothing you should have to request is shown up front.
  3. 3CPR for 2 minutes performs two minutes of CPR and shows the rhythm on the monitor. Skip time only advances the clock and does not count as an action. Check rhythm and pulse is a separate step.
  4. 4With Show solution off, feedback states only what was done and the objective event. It will not interpret the monitor or tell you what to do next.
  5. 5If a key step is overdue you will get escalating nudges. After the third nudge the case ends. The debrief names the missed step and the correct action; the live nudges stay vague.
  6. 6Call the reg gives a hint. Each call deducts 0.5 from the decision score, with a minimum of 0.
  7. 7Show solution is off by default. Turn it on for more guidance, including why the rhythm changed and the guideline link.
  8. 8The debrief shows your score, what you missed, and the guideline for each decision.
  9. 9A dangerous error (for example CPR or an unsynchronised shock in a patient with a pulse, or shocking asystole) ends the case immediately when Show solution is off, with the words Patient harmed: scenario ended. The debrief explains why. With Show solution on, it is marked Not quite and the case continues.
  10. 10Restart starts the same case again.

          How the strips are made

          Every strip is synthesised in the browser from simple wave components (P, QRS, T, flutter and fibrillatory baselines, pacing spikes) placed on a 10 second lead II timeline at 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV. A seeded random generator varies the rate and intervals within the ranges given for each rhythm. These are teaching approximations, not recordings: real ECGs are noisier and more varied. Bundle branch blocks also show synthetic V1 and V6 strips because one limb lead cannot diagnose them. Conduction diagrams are schematic and not to scale.
