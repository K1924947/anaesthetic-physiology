Close-up: lead II, two beats

The same paper speed and gain as the 12-lead (25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV), drawn larger so the shape of each wave is visible. A normal QRS is narrow: under 120 ms is only 3 small squares. Intervals are measured from the model across all 12 leads.

About this model

  • This is a simplified teaching model, not a patient recording. The heart is made of about 10,000 small muscle elements. The impulse starts at the SA node, spreads through the atria cell to cell, is held in the AV node, races down the His bundle, bundle branches and Purkinje fibres, then spreads through the ventricular wall from the inside (endocardium) outwards.
  • Each element adds a small dipole as its wavefront passes. Their sum at each moment is the mean electrical vector (the black arrow). The limb leads record the arrow's component along their own line; the chest leads are computed from the nearby muscle, because they sit close to the heart.
  • Conduction speeds follow published values for a real heart (atrial muscle about 0.5 m/s, AV node about 0.05 m/s, His and bundle branches about 2 m/s, Purkinje fibres about 4 m/s), scaled to the model. Shapes, sizes, the ventricular muscle speed and a slower His bundle (because the model heart is small, this gives a normal His-to-ventricle time of about 40 ms) are model choices, tuned so that the normal 12-lead falls within normal limits.
  • The limb leads use the hexaxial angles: I 0°, II +60°, aVF +90°, aVR -150° (positive angles point downwards). The augmented leads are drawn at 0.87 times the size of I and II. Chest electrode positions are schematic.
  • The model beat runs at 75 per minute.
