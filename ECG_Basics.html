Normal values at a glance

Where sources disagree, both figures are given. Those rows are flagged for review before teaching with this page.

Normal values for the main parts of an ECG beat, with the source for each figure
Part Represents Usual normal Source
P wave Atrial depolarisation LITFL: duration under 0.12 s; height under 2.5 mm in the limb leads. Klabunde: usual duration 0.08 to 0.10 s. LITFL, P wave; Klabunde
PR interval Start of P to start of QRS (atrial depolarisation plus AV delay) 0.12 to 0.20 s (3 to 5 small squares). Over 0.20 s: first degree AV block. LITFL, PR interval; Klabunde
QRS complex Ventricular depolarisation LITFL: 70 to 100 ms normal; over 100 ms abnormal; over 120 ms for bundle branch block or a ventricular rhythm. Klabunde: 0.06 to 0.10 s, prolonged if over 0.10 s. Often taught in the UK as under 0.12 s (Hampton, The ECG Made Easy; page not checked here). LITFL, QRS interval; Klabunde
ST segment Ventricles fully depolarised; roughly the action potential plateau Isoelectric, judged at the J point against the T-P segment. LITFL, ST segment; Klabunde
T wave Ventricular repolarisation Upright in all leads except aVR and V1. Height under 5 mm in the limb leads and under 10 mm in the chest leads (10 mm in men, 8 mm in women in the precordial leads). LITFL, T wave; Klabunde
QT and QTc Ventricular depolarisation and repolarisation, corrected for rate LITFL: QTc prolonged over 440 ms in men or 460 ms in women; over 500 ms linked to torsades; under 350 ms short. Klabunde: normal QTc 0.44 s or less. LITFL, QT interval; Klabunde
RR and rate Time from one R wave to the next; used to calculate rate LITFL (adults): normal 50 to 100 /min. Many texts still use 60 to 100 /min. At 25 mm/s: rate = 300 / large squares between R waves. LITFL, rate interpretation

The PR segment (the flat line between the P wave and the QRS) is covered on the labelled beat. The small U wave after the T wave is included there too, but it is not needed to read a basic ECG.

QTc, briefly

The uncorrected QT shortens as the heart rate rises, so we estimate what the QT would be at 60 /min. The most commonly used formula is Bazett: QTc = QT ÷ √RR, with RR in seconds (LITFL; Klabunde uses the same formula).

Bazett over-corrects above 100 /min and under-corrects below 60 /min. Outside 60 to 100 /min, LITFL prefers Fridericia or Framingham. A useful rule of thumb is that a normal QT is under half the preceding RR interval. Do not use a single QTc value alone to make clinical decisions.
